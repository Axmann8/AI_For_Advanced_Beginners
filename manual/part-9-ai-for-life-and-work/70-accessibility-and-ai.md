# 70 · Accessibility & AI: Technology That Includes Everyone ♿💜

> ⏱️ 8 min read · 🎯 Everyone: disabled people, families, caregivers, teachers and builders · 🧰 Needs: a phone (many tools are free), an assistant with vision and voice

**For many disabled people, AI isn't a novelty; it's independence.** An app that describes the world through a phone's camera,
live captions for every conversation, a synthetic voice built from your own recordings, a helper that breaks a daunting task
into tiny steps: these are life-changing. This chapter tours AI tools for vision, hearing, speech, mobility, and cognitive and
neurodivergent needs, then shows how *you* can build more accessible things. Nothing about us without us. 💜

<details class="eli5" open>
<summary>🧸 ELI5: This chapter in 30 seconds</summary>

Some people can't see, hear, speak, move or focus the way others do. AI can help: it can describe a picture out loud to
someone who can't see it, write down what people are saying for someone who can't hear, speak for someone who has lost their
voice, and turn big scary jobs into small easy steps. It helps everyone join in. 🤝🌈

</details>

## 🗺️ The accessibility map

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Different people need different kinds of help. Here's a map of which AI tools help with seeing, hearing, speaking, moving and
thinking.

</details>

| Need | AI can help with | Example tools |
|---|---|---|
| 👁️ **Blind & low vision** | Describing scenes, reading text, identifying objects | Be My Eyes (Be My AI), Seeing AI, Google Lookout, Envision, assistant apps with vision |
| 👂 **Deaf & hard of hearing** | Live captions, transcripts, sound alerts | Live Transcribe, Live Captions (phones and computers), captioned calls |
| 🗣️ **Speech** | Understanding atypical speech, synthetic voices | Project Relate, Personal Voice, voice banking and cloning |
| ✋ **Mobility & dexterity** | Voice control, fewer clicks, automation | Voice Control/Access, dictation, agents and automations |
| 🧠 **Cognitive & neurodivergent** | Task breakdown, simplified text, focus support | Goblin Tools, assistants with custom instructions |
| 📖 **Dyslexia & reading** | Read-aloud, simplified text, spelling help | Text-to-speech, reading modes, assistants |

> [!NOTE]
> **📌 Built into your devices**
> Phones and computers include powerful accessibility features (screen readers, magnifiers, live captions, voice control).
> Check **Settings → Accessibility** first: many tools are free and already there.

## 👁️ Blind & low-vision

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Point your phone camera at something and AI describes it out loud: what's in the photo, what the sign says, what color your
shirt is.

</details>

| Task | How |
|---|---|
| **Describe a scene** | Be My AI, Seeing AI or an assistant: *"Describe what's in front of me, starting with anything important for safety."* |
| **Read text** | Mail, menus, labels, screens: camera → read aloud |
| **Identify things** | *"Which of these cans is the tomatoes?"* *"Does this shirt match these trousers?"* |
| **Understand images online** | Share any image to an assistant for a description |
| **Navigate apps** | Describe a confusing screen from a screenshot |
| **Human backup** | Be My Eyes connects to sighted volunteers when AI isn't enough |

> [!WARNING]
> **⚠️ AI descriptions can be wrong**
> For safety-critical things (medication labels, traffic, expiry dates), double-check with a second tool or a person. AI can
> misread small print or miss details.

## 👂 Deaf & hard of hearing

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

AI can write down everything people say, like subtitles for real life, and let you know about important sounds like a doorbell
or a baby crying.

</details>

- **Live captions everywhere:** phones and computers can caption conversations, calls, videos and meetings in real time.
- **Meeting transcripts:** AI notes in Meet, Teams, Zoom and others, with searchable text afterwards.
- **Sound recognition:** alerts for doorbells, alarms, crying babies, running water.
- **Caption your own videos** so everyone can enjoy them ([Video & Audio Production](../part-8-creative-ai/54-video-and-audio-production.md#-dubbing-translation--accessibility)).
- **Speech-to-text for replies:** type your reply and have it spoken aloud.

## 🗣️ Speech & voice

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Some people speak in ways computers find hard to understand, or lose their voice because of an illness. AI can learn to
understand them, and can even save a copy of someone's voice so they can keep "speaking" in their own voice.

</details>

| Need | AI help |
|---|---|
| **Atypical speech** | Apps like Project Relate learn an individual's speech patterns for better recognition |
| **Voice banking** | Record your voice to create a personal synthetic voice (e.g. Apple's Personal Voice, and specialized programs for people with ALS/MND) |
| **Text-to-speech** | Natural voices for AAC (augmentative and alternative communication) |
| **Faster communication** | AI suggests likely phrases to speed up typing-to-speak |
| **Practice** | Voice-mode practice for speech therapy exercises (with a therapist's guidance) |

**Voice banking** is one of the most moving uses of AI: people facing voice loss can keep speaking in a voice that sounds like
themselves. 💜

## ✋ Mobility & dexterity

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

If using a mouse or keyboard is hard, you can control your computer and phone with your voice, and AI helpers can do long
clicky jobs for you.

</details>

- **Voice control:** built-in Voice Control (Apple) and Voice Access (Android and Windows) operate the whole device by voice.
- **Dictation everywhere:** tools like Wispr Flow turn speech into polished text.
- **Fewer clicks:** automations and agents handle repetitive multi-step tasks ([Automation Recipe Book](../part-3-automation/21-automation-recipe-book.md), [Computer Use](../part-5-building-with-ai/40-computer-use-and-browser-agents.md)).
- **Smart home:** voice-controlled lights, doors, blinds and thermostats ([Home, Cooking & DIY](69-home-cooking-and-diy.md#-the-ai-powered-smart-home)).
- **Coding by voice:** describe changes to a coding agent instead of typing lots of code.

## 🧠 Cognitive & neurodivergent support

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

For brains that find planning, starting tasks or reading long text hard, AI can break jobs into tiny steps, turn long text into
short simple text, and gently help you stay on track.

</details>

| Challenge | AI support | Prompt |
|---|---|---|
| **Starting tasks** | Break it into tiny steps | *"Break 'do my taxes' into steps so small they feel easy."* |
| **Time blindness** | Realistic estimates and timers | *"How long will each step really take? Add buffers."* |
| **Overwhelm** | Sort and prioritize | The [overwhelm reset](64-life-admin-and-productivity.md#-the-overwhelm-reset) |
| **Reading load** | Simplify and summarize | *"Rewrite this in plain language, short sentences, bullet points."* |
| **Tone uncertainty** | Check how messages come across | *"How might this email sound to the reader? Is anything unclear or too blunt?"* |
| **Social scripts** | Rehearse conversations | *"Help me prepare for a phone call to book a dentist appointment."* |
| **Working memory** | Checklists and reminders | *"Turn this into a checklist and remind me tomorrow."* |
| **Focus** | Body doubling | Voice-mode companion for work sessions |

**Goblin Tools** (a set of simple AI tools built for neurodivergent people) is a lovely example: a task breaker ("magic to-do"),
a tone judge, a formalizer and more.

> [!TIP]
> **💡 Put your needs in custom instructions**
> *"I have ADHD and dyslexia. Keep answers short, use bullet points and headings, one idea per line, and give me the next single
> step at the end."* Every answer then adapts to you, automatically.

## 👵 Older adults & tech confidence

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

AI can be a patient helper for grandparents learning new technology: explaining step by step, as many times as they need,
without ever getting impatient.

</details>

- **Patient tech help:** *"Explain how to video-call my grandkids on this phone, one step at a time. I'll tell you when I'm ready
  for the next step."*
- **Screenshots help:** share a screenshot of what's on the screen for exact guidance.
- **Scam checks:** *"Is this message a scam?"* ([Money & Personal Finance](65-money-and-personal-finance.md#-scams--ai-powered-fraud))
- **Larger, simpler:** ask for large-text, simple-language answers.
- **Family setup:** set up custom instructions for them (patient, step-by-step, no jargon).

## 🛠️ Building accessible things

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

When you make websites, videos or documents, AI can help make sure everyone can use them: describing pictures, adding captions,
and using clear words.

</details>

| Make it accessible | AI help |
|---|---|
| **Alt text for images** | *"Write concise, meaningful alt text for each image. Skip decorative ones."* |
| **Captions & transcripts** | Auto-captions + a human proofread |
| **Plain language** | *"Rewrite this for a reading age of 12 without losing meaning."* |
| **Color contrast** | *"Check these color pairs against WCAG AA."* ([Design & UI](../part-8-creative-ai/59-design-and-ui.md#-accessibility-design-for-everyone)) |
| **Code audits** | *"Audit this page for keyboard navigation, focus states, ARIA and screen-reader issues. Fix them."* |
| **Accessible documents** | Headings, lists, descriptive links, and readable PDFs |

**Remember:** AI audits are a great start but not a guarantee. The best test is **real users with disabilities**, and paying
disabled people for their expertise.

## 💜 Principles: respect, choice & privacy

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Disabled people are the experts on what they need. AI tools should help them do things their way, keep their information
private, and never make choices for them.

</details>

| Principle | In practice |
|---|---|
| **Nothing about us without us** | Involve disabled people in designing tools and content |
| **Choice and control** | AI should assist, not decide for people |
| **Privacy** | Camera descriptions and health info are sensitive, so check settings |
| **Reliability** | Critical tasks need backups (human help, second tools) |
| **Not a replacement for access** | AI helps, but ramps, interpreters, and accessible design are still essential |

## 🎯 Key takeaways

- For many disabled people, AI means **independence**: describing the world, captioning conversations, speaking and planning.
- Check **built-in accessibility settings** first: many powerful tools are free.
- **Voice banking**, **live captions** and **scene description** are among AI's most meaningful uses.
- **Custom instructions** adapt every answer to your needs.
- When building, use AI for **alt text, captions, plain language and audits**, and test with **real users**.

## 🧠 Check yourself

<details class="quiz">
<summary>❓ 1. What's voice banking?</summary>

Recording your voice so AI can create a **personal synthetic voice**, letting people facing voice loss keep speaking as
themselves.

</details>

<details class="quiz">
<summary>❓ 2. Why double-check AI scene descriptions for medication labels?</summary>

AI can **misread small print**. For safety-critical information, use a second tool or a person.

</details>

<details class="quiz">
<summary>❓ 3. How can you make every AI answer fit how your brain works?</summary>

Put your needs and preferences in **custom instructions** (format, length, style, next-step at the end).

</details>

> [!TIP]
> **🎮 Try this**
> Open **Settings → Accessibility** on your phone and turn on one feature you've never tried (live captions, magnifier, or a
> screen reader for 5 minutes). Then take a photo of something around you and ask an assistant to describe it. You'll see
> technology in a whole new way. 💜
