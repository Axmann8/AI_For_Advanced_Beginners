# 15 · Your First Week of Wins: 7 Mini-Projects 🏆

> ⏱️ 7 days × 20–30 min · 🎯 Complete beginners ready to practice · 🧰 Needs: any AI assistant (phone app recommended)

**Reading about AI is nice. Using it to make your real life easier is when it clicks.** Here are seven small projects,
one a day, each solving a genuine everyday problem while quietly teaching you a core AI skill. By Sunday you'll have
planned a week of meals, sent a tricky message, decoded a confusing document, learned something new, planned an outing,
made something creative and built your own personal AI helper. Let's go! 🚀

<details class="eli5" open>
<summary>🧸 ELI5: This chapter in 30 seconds</summary>

This is a one-week challenge with seven little missions. Each day you use AI to do something actually useful, like
planning dinners or writing a hard message. Each mission teaches you a new AI trick. Finish all seven and you're a real
AI user!

</details>

## 🍽️ Day 1: A week of meals and a shopping list

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Tell the AI who's eating, what you like and your budget, and it plans your dinners for the week and writes your shopping
list.

</details>

**Time:** 20 minutes · **Skill:** giving context and constraints

1. Tell the AI about your household:
   > *"Plan 5 weeknight dinners for [2 adults and a 10-year-old]. We like [pasta, curries and tacos], dislike
   > [mushrooms], and I have [30 minutes] to cook on weeknights. Budget-friendly. Ask me any questions first."*
2. Answer its questions.
3. Steer: *"Swap Wednesday for something vegetarian,"* *"Make Friday a fun one."*
4. Then: *"Make a shopping list grouped by supermarket section, and tell me what I can prep on Sunday."*
5. Bonus: *"Check my list against what I already have: [rice, pasta, eggs, onions]."*

🏅 **Level up:** snap a photo of your fridge and ask *"What can I make tonight with this?"*

**What you learned:** context (who, likes, time, budget) turns generic suggestions into a plan made for you.

## 📬 Day 2: Tame a tricky message

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Pick a message you've been putting off, like a complaint or an awkward "no," and let the AI help you get the words just
right.

</details>

**Time:** 20 minutes · **Skill:** steering tone with follow-ups

Think of a message you've been putting off: a complaint, a request, saying no, an apology, chasing money someone owes.

1. Explain the situation:
   > *"I need to [ask my neighbor to stop parking across my driveway]. We get on okay and I want to keep it friendly,
   > but it's happened 5 times. Draft a short text."*
2. Steer the tone: *"Warmer,"* *"A bit firmer,"* *"Shorter,"* *"Less formal: it's a text, not a letter."*
3. Ask: *"How might they react, and how should I respond to each reaction?"*
4. Edit the final version so it sounds like **you**, and send it (or save it for the right moment). 💪

**What you learned:** the first draft is a starting point; steering gets you exactly the right words.

## 🧾 Day 3: Decode a confusing document

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Find a confusing letter, bill or manual, show it to the AI, and ask it to explain it in simple words.

</details>

**Time:** 20 minutes · **Skill:** uploading files and asking precise questions

Find something confusing: a bill, an insurance letter, a school policy, an appliance manual, a lease.

1. **Remove private numbers** (account numbers, ID numbers), then upload a photo or PDF, or paste the text.
2. Ask:
   > *"Explain this in plain English. What is it about, what do I need to do, and by when?"*
3. Then precise questions:
   > *"Is there anything unusual or worth questioning here?"*
   > *"Answer only from this document: what's the fee if I cancel early?"*
4. If action is needed: *"Draft a short reply / list the steps I should take."*

**What you learned:** give AI the real material, ask specific questions, and say "only from this document" for
accuracy.

## 🎓 Day 4: Learn something in 20 minutes

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Pick something you've always wanted to understand, and let the AI teach you like a patient teacher, then quiz you to
check you got it.

</details>

**Time:** 20 minutes · **Skill:** using AI as a tutor

Pick something you've always wondered about: how the stock market works, why we have time zones, how to read music, the
basics of a language.

1. > *"Teach me the basics of [how the stock market works] in 5 short lessons. I'm a complete beginner. After each
   > lesson, ask me one question to check I understood, and wait for my answer."*
2. Answer honestly; say "I don't get it" whenever you don't. It'll explain differently.
3. At the end: *"Give me a one-page summary and 3 good sources to learn more."*
4. Try it in **voice mode** for part of it. It's like a private tutor on the phone.

**What you learned:** AI is an endlessly patient teacher, especially when it quizzes you instead of lecturing.

## 🗺️ Day 5: Plan an outing

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Plan a day out or a trip with the AI, then double-check the important details, like opening times, on the real websites.

</details>

**Time:** 30 minutes · **Skill:** planning with web search and fact-checking

1. > *"Plan a [Saturday day trip] from [Manchester] for [2 adults and a dog]. We like [countryside walks and a good
   > pub lunch]. Up to [1.5 hours] away. Search the web for current info and include links."*
2. Pick a favorite and ask: *"Make a timeline for the day with travel times."*
3. **Fact-check** (the important part!): open the links and check **opening hours, prices, dog rules** on the official
   websites. Tell the AI anything it got wrong.
4. Bonus: *"What should we pack, and what's the backup plan if it rains?"*

**What you learned:** AI plans brilliantly, and **you** verify the facts that matter
([When AI Gets It Wrong](10-when-ai-gets-it-wrong.md)).

## 🎨 Day 6: Make something creative

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Make something fun: a story, a poem or a birthday card, with a picture made by AI to go with it.

</details>

**Time:** 30 minutes · **Skill:** voice and image creation

Choose one:

- 🎂 **A custom card:** a funny poem for someone's birthday plus an AI image of their favorite things, printed or sent.
- 📖 **A bedtime story:** co-write a story with a child (*"You start, then my daughter decides what happens next!"*),
  then make an illustration for it.
- 🐾 **Pet portrait:** upload a photo of your pet and ask for it as a Renaissance painting, a superhero or a cartoon.
- 🎙️ **Voice challenge:** have a 10-minute spoken brainstorm with AI about a hobby project, then ask for a written
  summary.

**What you learned:** AI is a creative partner, and describing subject, style and mood gets great images
([chapter 9](09-voice-photos-and-files.md)).

## 🤖 Day 7: Build your own helper

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Make a special AI helper for something you do often, like a meal planner or a homework helper, so you don't have to
explain everything every time.

</details>

**Time:** 30 minutes · **Skill:** custom instructions and reusable assistants

Most assistants let you create a **reusable helper** with its own instructions:

| Assistant | Feature | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 💬 ChatGPT | **GPTs** or **Projects** | Sidebar → GPTs → Create, or New project |
| ✨ Gemini | **Gems** | Sidebar → Gems → New Gem |
| 🧡 Claude | **Projects** | Sidebar → Projects → New project → set instructions |
| 🔎 Perplexity | **Spaces** | Sidebar → Spaces |
| 🌬️ Le Chat | **Agents / Projects** | Sidebar |
| Any assistant | **A saved prompt** | Paste it at the start of a chat |

Build one for something you do often. Example instructions for a **Family Meal Planner**:

```text
You are our family meal planner.
- Family: 2 adults, kids aged 7 and 10. No mushrooms. One vegetarian night a week.
- Weeknights: 30 minutes max. Budget-friendly, normal supermarket ingredients.
- Always give: 5 dinners, a shopping list by aisle, and Sunday prep tips.
- Keep it cheerful and brief.
```

Other ideas: a **homework helper** that never gives answers, only hints; a **work email polisher** in your style; a
**garden advisor** that knows your climate and plot; a **language buddy** at your level.

**What you learned:** you can turn a great prompt into a **reusable assistant**. That's a big step toward advanced
beginner territory! 🎉

## 🎯 Key takeaways

- Learning sticks when you solve **real problems**: meals, messages, paperwork, learning, plans, creativity.
- Each mission teaches a **core skill**: context, steering, documents, tutoring, verifying, creating, reusing.
- **Reusable helpers** (GPTs, Gems, Projects, Spaces) save you from repeating yourself.
- Keep verifying facts that matter, especially times, prices and rules.
