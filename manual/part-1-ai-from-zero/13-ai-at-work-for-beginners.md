# 13 · AI at Work for Beginners 💼

> ⏱️ 15 min read · 🎯 Anyone with a job (or looking for one) · 🧰 Needs: your workplace's approved AI tool, or any assistant for practice

**AI can take the grind out of work: the email you've been dreading, the meeting notes nobody wrote up, the spreadsheet
formula you can never remember, the report that needs to be half as long by 5pm.** This chapter shows beginners how to
use AI at work safely and well, with ready-made prompts for emails, meetings, documents and spreadsheets, ideas for
every kind of job, and the rules that keep you out of trouble.

<details class="eli5" open>
<summary>🧸 ELI5: This chapter in 30 seconds</summary>

At work, AI is like a super-fast helper for the boring bits: writing emails, tidying notes, explaining spreadsheets and
getting ready for tricky conversations. But work has rules: some information is secret, so first check which AI tools
your job allows, and always read what the AI wrote before you send it.

</details>

## 📜 Step zero: check the rules

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Before using AI for work, find out which AI tools your job allows and what information you're allowed to put in them.

</details>

Before pasting anything work-related into an AI, find out:

1. **Which tools are approved?** Many employers provide a business version of Copilot, Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude with
   stronger privacy protections. Use that one.
2. **What data is allowed?** Customer details, health records, financial data and unreleased plans are usually
   off-limits in personal AI accounts.
3. **Is there an AI policy?** Ask your manager or IT. If there's none, suggest one. You'll look like a leader. 😉

> [!WARNING]
> **⚠️ Personal accounts + company secrets = trouble**
> Pasting confidential documents into a free personal chatbot can break company rules or even laws (like data
> protection rules). When in doubt, **remove names and specifics**, or use the approved business tool.

## 📧 Email superpowers

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

AI can write emails for you, make grumpy emails polite, and turn long email chains into a short summary.

</details>

| Task | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Write from bullet points | *"Turn these notes into a clear, friendly email to the team: [notes]."* |
| Tricky reply | *"Here's an angry email from a customer. Draft a calm, helpful reply that apologizes without admitting legal fault: [email]."* |
| Tone check | *"Does this email sound rude or passive-aggressive? Suggest a better version: [email]."* |
| Say no nicely | *"Help me decline this extra project politely. I'm at capacity until March."* |
| Summarize a thread | *"Summarize this email thread: what was decided, what's still open, and what I need to do."* |
| Follow up | *"Write a short, polite follow-up. They haven't replied in a week."* |

**Built-in helpers:** Gmail has **Gemini** ("Help me write," thread summaries), Outlook has **Copilot** ("Draft with
Copilot," "Summarize"), and Apple Mail has **Writing Tools**. Details in [Email & Calendar
Superpowers](../part-6-ai-in-your-apps/57-email-and-calendar.md).

## 🗓️ Meetings: before, during, after

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

AI can help you prepare for meetings, take notes during them, and write up who needs to do what afterwards.

</details>

- **Before:** *"I have a meeting with [a new supplier] about [delivery delays]. Suggest an agenda, 5 questions to ask,
  and how to open the conversation."*
- **During:** Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet all offer **AI note-takers** that write summaries and action items
  (if your company enables them). **Always tell attendees** when AI is recording or taking notes.
- **After:** *"Turn these rough notes into minutes with decisions, action items (with owners) and deadlines."*
- **Missed it?** *"Summarize this transcript for someone who missed the meeting. Just what they need to know."*

## 📝 Documents, reports and presentations

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

AI can help you plan a document, write a first draft, make long things shorter and turn notes into slides.

</details>

- **Outline first:** *"I need a 2-page report on [why customer complaints rose in Q2]. Suggest a structure."*
- **Summarize:** *"Summarize this 20-page policy into one page for frontline staff."*
- **Rewrite for an audience:** *"Rewrite this technical update for non-technical managers."*
- **Slides:** *"Turn this report into a 6-slide outline with a title and 3 bullets per slide."* Copilot in PowerPoint
  and Gemini in Google Slides can build the slides themselves.
- **Proofread:** *"Fix grammar and clarity but keep my style. List what you changed."*

## 📊 Spreadsheets without the fear

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Spreadsheets can be scary. AI can write the formulas for you and explain what your numbers mean.

</details>

You don't need to be an Excel wizard anymore:

- *"Write an Excel formula that [counts how many rows in column C say 'Late']. Explain how to use it."*
- *"What does this formula do? `=IFERROR(VLOOKUP(A2,Sheet2!A:B,2,FALSE),"")`"*
- *"I've attached our sales data. Which products are growing, which are shrinking, and anything surprising?"*
- *"Make me a simple spreadsheet layout to track [staff holidays]."*

Copilot in Excel and Gemini in Google Sheets can do much of this inside the spreadsheet. More in
[Spreadsheet Superpowers](../part-6-ai-in-your-apps/58-spreadsheet-superpowers.md).

## 🧠 A thinking partner for tricky moments

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

AI is great for practicing hard conversations and thinking through problems, like having a wise coworker to talk to.

</details>

- **Practice a hard conversation:** *"Role-play my manager. I'm asking for a raise. Push back realistically, then give
  me feedback."*
- **Prepare feedback:** *"Help me give constructive feedback to a team member who keeps missing deadlines. Kind but
  clear."*
- **Make a decision:** *"Help me weigh up [two job offers]. Ask me questions, then summarize pros and cons."*
- **Learn fast:** *"I just started in [logistics]. Explain the 15 terms I'll hear most, simply."*
- **Plan your week:** *"Here's everything on my plate: [list]. Help me prioritize and plan the week."*

## 🛠️ Ideas for every kind of job

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

AI isn't just for office jobs. Here are ideas for shops, schools, hospitals, trades and more.

</details>

| Job | Ways AI helps |
|---|---|
| 🛒 **Retail & hospitality** | Staff rotas, menu descriptions, replies to online reviews, training checklists |
| 🏥 **Healthcare admin** | Patient letter templates (no real patient data in personal tools!), explaining procedures in plain language |
| 🍎 **Teaching** | Lesson ideas, differentiated worksheets, parent emails, quiz questions |
| 🔧 **Trades** | Quotes and invoices, explaining work to customers, safety checklists, marketing posts |
| 💼 **Office & admin** | Emails, minutes, reports, spreadsheets, process documents |
| 📞 **Customer service** | Reply drafts, calming templates for angry customers, FAQ pages |
| 🎨 **Creative work** | Brainstorming, first drafts, feedback, mood boards |
| 🚚 **Logistics & field work** | Route and schedule planning, incident reports from voice notes |
| 🧑‍💼 **Small business owners** | Everything above, plus social posts, product descriptions and business plans (see [Small Business](../part-11-ai-for-life-and-work/93-small-business.md)) |

## ⚠️ Five rules for using AI at work

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Five simple rules: follow company rules, keep secrets secret, check the facts, make it sound like you, and be honest
about using AI.

</details>

1. **Use approved tools** for work data. Keep secrets out of personal accounts.
2. **You're responsible for what you send.** Read every word; check facts, figures and names.
3. **Make it sound like you.** Edit AI drafts so they're in your voice. Colleagues notice the robotic "I hope this email
   finds you well" style. 😅
4. **Be honest** about AI use where it matters (for example, if your workplace or clients expect disclosure).
5. **Keep learning the job, not just the tool.** Use AI to go faster, not to skip understanding your work.

## 📈 AI skills are career skills

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Knowing how to use AI well is becoming a skill that helps you get jobs and do better at work.

</details>

Being good with AI is quickly becoming as expected as being good with email. The people who thrive are the ones who
**use AI to do better work**, not the ones who avoid it or hand it everything. Start small: one email, one meeting
summary, one formula. In a month, you'll wonder how you managed without it.

## 🎯 Key takeaways

- **Check your workplace's rules** and use approved tools for work data.
- AI shines at **emails, meeting notes, documents, spreadsheets** and as a **thinking partner**.
- Many workplace apps (Outlook, Gmail, Word, Docs, Excel, Sheets, Zoom, Teams, Meet) have **AI built in**.
- **You own what you send:** read, check and make it sound like you.
- AI skills are **career skills**. Start with one small task a day.

## 🧠 Check yourself

<details class="quiz">
<summary>❓ 1. You want AI to summarize a spreadsheet of customer names and complaints. What should you check first?</summary>

Whether your company **allows** that data in the tool you're using. Use the **approved business AI**, or remove
customer names first.

</details>

<details class="quiz">
<summary>❓ 2. The AI drafted a great email to a client. Can you send it straight away?</summary>

Read it first: check **facts, names, numbers and tone**, and make it sound like **you**. You're responsible for what you
send.

</details>

<details class="quiz">
<summary>❓ 3. You're using an AI note-taker in a meeting. What's the courteous (and often legally required) step?</summary>

**Tell the attendees** that AI is recording or taking notes.

</details>

> [!TIP]
> **🎮 Try this**
> Pick the **most boring recurring task** in your work week (a report, a summary email, a rota). Next time it comes up,
> ask AI to do the first draft using the prompts above, then edit it. Time yourself. Most people save 15–60 minutes on
> the very first try. ⏱️
