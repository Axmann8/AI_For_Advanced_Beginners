# 45 · Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) Masterclass 🎧📓

> ⏱️ 8 min read · 🎯 Everyone (students, researchers, curious people) · 🧰 Needs: a Google account (some features depend on your plan)

**Google's research notebook is one of the most delightful AI tools ever made.** Upload a pile of sources, and it becomes an
expert on *exactly* that material: every answer cites your sources, and one click turns them into a podcast, a video, a mind
map, flashcards, a quiz or a slide deck. It started life as **NotebookLM** and was renamed **Gemini Notebook** in July 2026.
Same product, same notebooks, bigger ambitions. This chapter covers everything it can do, plus workflows for studying,
research, work and fun. 🎉

> [!NOTE]
> **📌 NotebookLM = Gemini Notebook**
> Google renamed NotebookLM to **Gemini Notebook** on July 16, 2026. Existing notebooks and shared links keep working, and
> you'll still see both names around the web (and in this manual). Features and limits vary by plan, so check the app for
> what yours includes.

<details class="eli5" open>
<summary>🧸 ELI5: This chapter in 30 seconds</summary>

Imagine a study buddy who reads *only* the books you hand them, remembers every page, and always points to the page an answer
came from. Then they can turn your whole stack of books into a fun radio show, a cartoon explainer, flashcards or a quiz.
That's Gemini Notebook. It's grounded, which means it sticks to your sources instead of making things up.

</details>

## ✨ Why it's special

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Most AI chats answer from everything they've ever read. This one answers only from your sources and shows you where, so you
can trust it and check it.

</details>

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| 📚 **Grounded in your sources** | Answers come from what *you* uploaded, which means far fewer hallucinations |
| 🔗 **Inline citations** | Click a citation number to jump to the exact passage |
| 🎧 **Audio Overviews** | Your sources become a lively podcast with two AI hosts |
| 🎬 **Video Overviews** | Narrated, illustrated explainer videos |
| 🧰 **The Studio** | Mind maps, reports, study guides, flashcards, quizzes, infographics, slide decks |
| 🔎 **Source discovery** | It can research the web and suggest sources for your topic |
| 💻 **Code execution** (rolling out on paid plans) | A secure cloud computer per notebook for data analysis grounded in your sources |
| 🔄 **Gemini app integration** | Attach a notebook to a Gemini chat as grounded knowledge |

## 📥 Adding sources

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

You feed it your materials: files, websites, videos and notes. The more focused your pile, the smarter your study buddy gets.

</details>

| Source type | Examples |
|---|---|
| 📄 **Documents** | PDFs, Google Docs, Google Slides, text and Markdown files |
| 🌐 **Websites** | Paste URLs of articles, docs pages, blog posts |
| ▶️ **YouTube** | Paste a video URL and it uses the transcript |
| 🎙️ **Audio** | Recordings of lectures, meetings, voice memos |
| 📋 **Pasted text** | Notes, emails, transcripts |
| 🔎 **Discovered sources** | Describe a topic and let it find and import web sources |

**Tips for great notebooks:**

- **One topic per notebook.** "Spanish class," "Kitchen renovation," "Job hunt 2026."
- **Quality over quantity:** 10 excellent sources beat 50 random ones.
- **Name your sources** clearly so citations are easy to understand.
- **Toggle sources on and off** to focus a question on specific ones.
- **Save good answers as notes** to build up your own synthesis.

## 💬 Chatting with your sources

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Ask questions like you would a teacher who's read everything in the pile. It answers and shows you exactly where each fact
came from.

</details>

| Great questions | Why they work |
|---|---|
| *"What are the 5 most important ideas across all sources?"* | Synthesis across documents |
| *"Where do these sources disagree?"* | Finds contradictions you'd miss |
| *"Explain [concept] like I'm 12, then like I'm an expert."* | Two levels of understanding |
| *"What questions would an examiner ask about chapter 3?"* | Exam prep |
| *"Make a timeline of every event mentioned."* | Structure from chaos |
| *"What's missing? What would a skeptic say these sources don't cover?"* | Finds gaps for more research |
| *"Quote the exact passage that supports this claim."* | Verification |

You can also set a **custom chat style** (for example: "Act as a Socratic tutor. Ask me questions instead of giving
answers.") for learning mode. 🧑‍🏫

## 🎧 Audio Overviews: the podcast machine

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

With one click, two friendly AI hosts turn your documents into a radio show that explains everything. You can even jump in
and ask them questions.

</details>

Audio Overviews turn your sources into a conversation between two AI hosts. It's the fastest "wow" in this whole manual. 🤯

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Formats** | Deep dive, a quick brief, a critique, or a debate between the hosts |
| **Length** | Shorter or longer versions |
| **Customize** | Tell the hosts who the audience is and what to focus on |
| **Languages** | Generate overviews in many languages |
| **Interactive mode** | "Join" the conversation and ask the hosts questions out loud |

**Customization prompts that shine:**

- *"The audience is a 10-year-old who loves dinosaurs. Keep it playful."*
- *"Focus only on the practical steps. Skip the history."*
- *"Debate format: one host argues for renting, the other for buying."*
- *"Explain this to me as a nervous first-time manager."*

## 🧰 The Studio: turn sources into anything

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

The Studio is a craft table. The same pile of sources can become a video, a map of ideas, flashcards, a quiz, a report or
slides, whatever helps you learn.

</details>

| Output | Best for |
|---|---|
| 🎬 **Video Overview** | Visual learners, sharing with others |
| 🧠 **Mind Map** | Seeing how ideas connect, then clicking a node to dive in |
| 📑 **Reports** (briefing doc, study guide, blog post, custom) | Summaries you can share or edit |
| 🃏 **Flashcards** | Memorizing, grounded in your material |
| ❓ **Quizzes** | Testing yourself before the real test |
| 📊 **Infographics** | One-page visual summaries |
| 🖼️ **Slide decks** | A presentation from your research |

Most outputs accept **instructions** ("focus on chapter 4," "make it harder," "for a board meeting") and can be **edited**
after they're generated.

## 🎓 Workflow: studying anything

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Put all your class stuff in one notebook, listen to the podcast on the bus, test yourself with flashcards and quizzes, and ask
about anything confusing.

</details>

1. **Create a notebook per course** and add lecture slides, readings, your notes and recorded lectures.
2. **Listen** to an Audio Overview on your commute. 🚌
3. **Map it:** generate a Mind Map to see the big picture.
4. **Test yourself** with Flashcards and a Quiz. Ask for harder ones as you improve.
5. **Tutor mode:** *"Quiz me one question at a time. Don't give answers until I try."*
6. **Before the exam:** *"Make a one-page study guide of what I keep getting wrong."*

More in [Research & Learning](../part-9-ai-for-life-and-work/60-research-and-learning.md) and [Parents, Teachers & Students](../part-9-ai-for-life-and-work/67-parents-teachers-and-students.md).

## 🔬 Workflow: research & writing

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

For a big project, collect good sources, find what they agree and disagree on, and write your own piece with citations you
can check.

</details>

1. **Discover sources:** describe your topic and import the best suggestions, plus your own finds.
2. **Map the territory:** *"Group these sources by viewpoint. Where do they disagree?"*
3. **Find gaps:** *"What important questions do these sources not answer?"* Then add sources that do.
4. **Outline:** *"Draft an outline for an article on X, citing sources for each section."*
5. **Write** (yourself, or in Claude with the notebook's report as context), then **fact-check** claims against the notebook.

## 💼 Workflow: work & life

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Notebooks aren't just for school. Use them for work projects, big life decisions, hobbies and family stuff.

</details>

| Notebook | Sources | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| 🏠 **Buying a home** | Listings, inspection reports, mortgage docs | *"What are the red flags across these inspection reports?"* |
| 💼 **Job hunt** | Your résumé, 5 job posts, company blogs | Audio Overview: *"How does this candidate fit these roles?"* |
| 🏥 **Understanding a diagnosis** | Reputable articles, your doctor's handouts | *"Questions to ask at my next appointment"* (always confirm with your doctor) |
| ✈️ **Trip planning** | Travel guides, blog posts, YouTube vlogs | *"A 5-day itinerary for someone who loves food and hates crowds"* |
| 🧾 **Company onboarding** | Handbooks, policies, team docs | *"What do I need to do in my first week?"* |
| 🎲 **Game night** | Rulebooks for 5 board games | *"How do you score in Wingspan again?"* |
| 🏛️ **Local politics** | Candidate websites, debate transcripts | *"Compare candidates' positions on housing"* |

## 🔌 Gemini Notebook in your wider toolkit

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Your notebooks can now be used from the Gemini app too, and you can move what you learn into your other tools.

</details>

- **Gemini app:** attach a notebook to a Gemini chat to ground the answers in your sources.
- **Google Drive & Workspace:** add Docs and Slides as sources, and use Workspace automations to refresh sources on a schedule.
- **Export:** copy reports into Docs, Notion or Obsidian ([Personal Knowledge Management](46-personal-knowledge-management.md)).
- **Pair with Claude:** use the notebook for grounded research, then take the report into Claude for writing or building.
- **Share** notebooks with classmates or teammates (view or edit), or publish them publicly.

## 🪤 Limits & tips

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

It's amazing, but it only knows your sources, it can still make small mistakes, and private stuff should be handled with care.

</details>

| Watch out for | Tip |
|---|---|
| **Only as good as your sources** | Add reputable, relevant sources |
| **Hosts can oversimplify** or occasionally misstate details | Check important claims against the cited passages |
| **Not a live web search** (outside source discovery) | Add fresh sources when things change |
| **Plan limits** on notebooks, sources and generations | Check your plan, and archive old notebooks |
| **Privacy** | Follow your school's or workplace's AI policy for sensitive documents ([Privacy & Your Data](../part-10-mastery/73-privacy-and-your-data.md)) |
| **Copyright** | Sharing generated podcasts of others' content publicly may raise rights issues |

## 🎯 Key takeaways

- **Gemini Notebook** (formerly NotebookLM) answers **only from your sources**, with **clickable citations**.
- **Audio and Video Overviews** turn documents into podcasts and explainers. Customize the audience and focus.
- The **Studio** makes mind maps, reports, flashcards, quizzes, infographics and slides.
- One **focused notebook per topic** beats one giant pile.
- Use it for **studying, research, work and life decisions**, and always check key claims.
