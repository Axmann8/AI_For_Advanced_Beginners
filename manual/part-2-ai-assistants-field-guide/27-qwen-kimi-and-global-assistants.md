# 27 · Qwen, Kimi & the Global Assistants 🌏

> ⏱️ 6 min read · 🎯 Curious explorers, multilingual users and travelers · 🧰 Needs: nothing (free accounts for the assistants you try)

**The AI world isn't just Silicon Valley.** Some of the most impressive (and most openly shared) AI models now come from
China, and there are excellent assistants built for Europe, India, Korea, the Middle East and beyond. This chapter is
your passport: Qwen, Kimi, Z.ai, MiniMax and friends, what they're great at, how to try them, and the privacy rules of
the road. 🛂

<details class="keypoints" open>
<summary>✅ Key Points & Steps</summary>

Strong AI assistants now come from around the world, not only the United States. Chinese labs such as Alibaba (Qwen) and Moonshot AI (Kimi) offer capable, often free assistants with openly published models, and many regions have assistants built for their own languages.

- **Benefits:** generous free tiers, open models and strong support for many languages.
- **Notable options:** Qwen, Kimi, Z.ai, MiniMax, and regional assistants in Europe, India, Korea and the Middle East.
- **Privacy:** your chats are subject to the laws where each company stores data, so choose what you share accordingly.

</details>

## 🌍 Why look beyond the big names?

<details class="keypoints" open>
<summary>✅ Key Points & Steps</summary>

Assistants beyond the best-known names can offer generous free tiers, better support for your language, or open models you can run and customize yourself.

</details>

- 🆓 **Generous free tiers:** several offer powerful features at no cost.
- 🔓 **Open weights:** many Chinese labs publish their models openly, so you can run them on your own computer or
  through providers you trust ([Local & Open Models](../part-9-local-ai/78-local-and-open-models.md)).
- 🗣️ **Languages and culture:** regional assistants often understand local languages, idioms and contexts better.
- 🧪 **Innovation:** long-document handling, agent modes and slide/website builders often appear here first.
- ✈️ **Travel:** in mainland China, most Western assistants aren't available; locals use Doubao, Qwen, DeepSeek, Kimi and
  others.

## 🟣 Qwen (Alibaba)

<details class="keypoints" open>
<summary>✅ Key Points & Steps</summary>

Qwen is Alibaba's assistant. It supports 119 languages, understands images and video, generates images, and is built on models Alibaba publishes openly.

</details>

| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | chat.qwen.ai (Qwen Chat) and the Qwen app |
| **Standouts** | Supports **119 languages**, image and video understanding, image generation, document processing, voice chat, deep research, and a web-building mode |
| **Open models** | The Qwen family is among the world's most-downloaded open models (the Qwen 3.x series runs locally with Ollama) |
| **Great for** | Multilingual tasks, translation, trying a wide range of model sizes, running AI locally |

**Try:** *"Translate this paragraph into Portuguese, Swahili and Vietnamese, and flag any phrases that don't translate
well culturally."*

<details class="keypoints" open>

</details>

| | |
|---|---|

## 🔐 Privacy: know where your words go

<details class="keypoints" open>
<summary>✅ Key Points & Steps</summary>

Every assistant is subject to the laws of the country where it stores data. Chinese services may be required to share data with authorities, so keep personal and work information out of them, or run their open models locally instead.

</details>

The same rule applies to every assistant: **your chats are subject to the laws where the company stores them.**

- **Chinese services** (Qwen, Kimi, Z.ai, Doubao, DeepSeek and others) generally store data in China, where companies can
  be required to share data with authorities. Great for general knowledge, coding practice and creative play; **not** for
  personal, financial, health or work-confidential information.
- **The open-weight loophole:** run these models **locally** or via a provider in your own country and your data never
  goes to the original company.
- **Content rules:** assistants follow local laws. Chinese assistants avoid politically sensitive topics; other countries
  have their own rules.
- **Work and school:** check your organization's policy first. Many restrict foreign AI services.

## 🍳 Step-by-step recipes

<details class="keypoints" open>
<summary>✅ Key Points & Steps</summary>

These three recipes offer practical ways to try global assistants, such as comparing how well each handles a second language.

</details>

**Recipe 1: The multilingual taste test**

1. Ask Qwen, your main assistant and Le Chat: *"Write a short, warm birthday message in [your second language] with
   natural, everyday phrasing."*
2. Ask a native speaker which sounds most natural.

**Recipe 2: A long-document challenge**

1. Upload the same long PDF to Kimi and your main assistant.
2. *"What are the three most surprising findings, with page references?"* Check the pages.

**Recipe 3: Run a global model at home**

1. Install **Ollama** → `ollama run qwen3.6:27b` (or a smaller size for modest computers).
2. Ask it something offline. Your data never leaves your computer. 🏠

## 🎯 Key takeaways

- **Qwen** (multilingual, open models), **Kimi** (long documents, agents, slides), **Z.ai**, **MiniMax**, **Doubao**
  and **ERNIE** are major Chinese assistants; many publish **open weights**.
- **Regional assistants** in Europe, India, Korea, the Middle East and Africa serve local languages and needs.
- Mind **jurisdiction**: keep sensitive information out of foreign services, or run open models **locally**.

<details class="quiz">
<summary>❓ 1. What's the private way to use a Chinese open-weight model like Qwen?</summary>

Run it **locally** (for example with Ollama) or through a **trusted provider in your country**, so data never goes to the
original company.

</details>

</details>
