# 77 · Teaching Others About AI: Pass the Magic On 🧑‍🏫✨

> ⏱️ 7 min read · 🎯 Anyone who's become "the AI person" among friends, family or coworkers · 🧰 Needs: a laptop or phone, a projector for workshops (optional), and patience

**You've learned a lot. Now you're probably the person everyone asks "wait, how did you do that?"** Teaching others is one of the
most rewarding things you can do with your new skills, and one of the best ways to deepen them. This chapter gives you a
15-minute first-demo recipe, a bank of simple analogies, ways to meet skeptics, worriers and enthusiasts where they are, honest
answers to common fears, workshop outlines, and tips for building an AI-friendly culture at work. 🌱➡️🌳

<details class="eli5" open>
<summary>🧸 ELI5: This chapter in 30 seconds</summary>

When you learn a cool magic trick, it's fun to teach your friends! Teaching AI works best when you show people something that
helps **them** (not you), use simple comparisons they understand, answer their worries honestly, and let them try it themselves
right away. Soon they'll be teaching others too. 🪄👫

</details>

## 🎯 The golden rule: start with *their* problem

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Don't show people the coolest trick; show them the trick that fixes something annoying in *their* life.

</details>

The fastest way to lose someone is a demo about *your* interests. The fastest way to win them is solving **their** annoying
problem in front of them.

| Person | Their problem | Your demo |
|---|---|---|
| 👵 **Grandparent** | Confusing letters, tech troubles | Photo of a letter → plain-English explanation |
| 🧑‍🍳 **Busy parent** | "What's for dinner?" | Fridge photo → 3 recipes + grocery list |
| 💼 **Coworker** | Long email threads, meeting notes | Thread → summary + action items |
| 🧑‍🎓 **Student** | Exam stress | Notes → quiz, tutor mode |
| 🏪 **Small business owner** | Social media dread | A month of posts from a brand brief |
| 🎸 **Hobbyist** | Wants to learn a song | Chords, practice plan, slowed-down stems |

## ⏱️ The 15-minute first demo

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

In 15 minutes: ask what bugs them, show AI fixing it, let them try, and give them one easy thing to do at home.

</details>

| Minute | Step | Tips |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | **Ask:** *"What's a boring task you'd love to hand off?"* | Listen. Pick something small and real |
| 2–6 | **Show** AI doing it | Think out loud: explain *why* you phrase things a certain way |
| 6–12 | **Let them drive** | Hands on keyboard (or voice). Coach, don't grab |
| 12–14 | **Show one "wow"** related to their interests | Voice mode, image understanding, or a quick Artifact |
| 14–15 | **Give homework** | One specific thing to try this week, written down |

> [!TIP]
> **💡 Let them type**
> People remember what they *do*, not what they watch. Even if it's slower, put their hands on the keyboard.

## 🎭 Meeting people where they are

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Some people are excited about AI, some are scared, and some think it's silly. Talk to each kind of person in a way that fits.

</details>

| Person | What they need | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| 😒 **The skeptic** ("it's just hype") | Proof on something real | Solve their actual task. Admit the limits honestly |
| 😟 **The worrier** ("it's scary") | Safety and control | Start with low-stakes tasks, show privacy settings |
| 🤩 **The enthusiast** ("it'll do everything!") | Realistic expectations | Show verification habits and limits |
| 😵 **The overwhelmed** ("too many tools!") | One simple starting point | One app, one use case, one week |
| 👴 **The late adopter** | Patience and confidence | Large text, voice mode, step-by-step, lots of praise |
| 🧑‍💻 **The techie** | Depth | Point them to MCP, agents and building chapters |

## 💬 Honest answers to common fears

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

People worry about jobs, privacy, cheating and the planet. Answer honestly: don't pretend AI is perfect, but share what helps.

</details>

| Worry | An honest, hopeful answer |
|---|---|
| **"Will it take my job?"** | "It changes many jobs, and people who learn to use it well are in a much stronger position. Let's find the boring parts of your job it can help with." ([Where This Is Heading](80-where-this-is-heading.md)) |
| **"Is my data safe?"** | "It depends on the settings and what you share. Let me show you the privacy switches and the traffic-light rule." ([Privacy & Your Data](73-privacy-and-your-data.md)) |
| **"Isn't it just cheating?"** | "It can be, if it replaces your thinking. Used as a tutor or editor, it makes you better." ([Research & Learning](../part-9-ai-for-life-and-work/60-research-and-learning.md)) |
| **"It gets things wrong!"** | "Yes! That's why we check important things. Let me show you how." |
| **"What about the environment?"** | "It uses real energy. Using right-sized models and avoiding waste helps." |
| **"It's too complicated."** | "You already know how to ask questions. That's 80% of it." |
| **"Is it conscious?"** | "Scientists and philosophers are still debating what's going on inside these systems. For practical use, treat it as a powerful tool that can be wrong." |

## 🏫 Workshop outlines

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

Ready-made plans for teaching a group: a lunch session at work, a family afternoon, or a community class.

</details>

=== "🍱 Lunch-and-learn (60 min)"

    | Time | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | 0–10 | "What would you hand off?" Sticky-note wall |
    | 10–25 | Live demo: 3 real team tasks (summary, draft, analysis) |
    | 25–45 | Hands-on in pairs with a printed prompt card |
    | 45–55 | Share wins, discuss privacy and company policy |
    | 55–60 | One task each to try this week |

=== "👨‍👩‍👧 Family afternoon (45 min)"

    | Time | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | 0–10 | Make a silly story together (kids choose the hero) |
    | 10–20 | "Catch the AI": find the mistakes game |
    | 20–35 | Plan something real: a trip, a party, a meal |
    | 35–45 | Family AI rules: what's OK, what's private ([Parents, Teachers & Students](../part-9-ai-for-life-and-work/67-parents-teachers-and-students.md)) |

=== "🏘️ Community class (90 min)"

    | Time | Activity |
    |---|---|
    | 0–15 | What AI is (analogy bank) and what it isn't |
    | 15–35 | Demos: letters, recipes, travel, scam checking |
    | 35–70 | Hands-on stations with helpers |
    | 70–85 | Safety and privacy: the traffic-light rule, scam red flags |
    | 85–90 | Take-home prompt card and resources |

**Make materials with AI:** *"Create a one-page, large-print prompt card for beginners with 10 useful prompts for everyday
life."* 🖨️

## 💼 Building an AI-friendly culture at work

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

At work, help everyone learn together: share good prompts, celebrate wins, and make clear, simple rules.

</details>

| Practice | How |
|---|---|
| **AI champions** | A few enthusiasts per team who help others |
| **Shared prompt library** | A Notion page or skills repository of prompts that work ([Prompt Library](../appendices/d-prompt-library.md)) |
| **Show-and-tell** | 10 minutes in team meetings: "one AI win this week" |
| **Clear policy** | Approved tools, data rules, disclosure, in plain language |
| **Safe sandbox** | A place to experiment without risk |
| **Measure wins** | Time saved, quality improved, happier customers |
| **Include everyone** | Accessible training, no shaming for questions |

## 📈 Measuring whether it worked

<details class="eli5">
<summary>🧸 ELI5</summary>

You'll know your teaching worked when people keep using AI on their own and start teaching others.

</details>

- They **used it again** within a week (the real test!).
- They can **explain one limitation** (hallucinations, privacy).
- They **found their own use case** you didn't show them.
- They **taught someone else**. 🎉

## 🎯 Key takeaways

- Start with **their** problem, not your favorite trick.
- The **15-minute demo**: ask, show, let them drive, one wow, homework.
- Use **simple analogies** (the ELI5 boxes in this manual are ready-made).
- Meet **skeptics, worriers and enthusiasts** where they are, and answer fears **honestly**.
- At work: **champions, shared prompts, clear policy, celebrated wins**.

## 🧠 Check yourself

<details class="quiz">
<summary>❓ 1. What's the most important principle for a first AI demo?</summary>

Solve **their real problem**, not show off your favorite trick, and **let them drive**.

</details>

<details class="quiz">
<summary>❓ 2. Someone says "AI will take my job." What's a helpful, honest response?</summary>

Acknowledge that AI is **changing many jobs**, then help them find tasks in *their* work where AI helps, since people who use it
well are better positioned.

</details>

<details class="quiz">
<summary>❓ 3. How do you know your teaching worked?</summary>

They **use it again on their own**, can **explain a limitation**, find **their own use cases**, and ideally **teach someone else**.

</details>
