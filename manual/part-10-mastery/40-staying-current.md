# 40 · Staying Current Without Drowning 🌊📰

AI moves at a ridiculous pace. New models, tools, and features launch every week. You don't need to follow
everything (nobody can!). You need a **light, reliable system** that surfaces what matters and helps you keep learning.

---

## The "sustainable AI diet" 🥗

| Frequency | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | 5 min | Skim one AI newsletter or your automated digest |
| **Weekly** | 30–60 min | Try **one** new thing hands-on (a tool, feature, or MCP server) |
| **Monthly** | 1–2 hours | A deeper learning session: a course module, a long read, or a project |
| **Quarterly** | 1 hour | Re-evaluate your stack ([Ch. 3](../part-1-foundations/03-choosing-your-ai-stack.md)) and re-run your personal eval ([Ch. 38](38-evaluating-ai.md)) |

**Rule:** *try > read.* Ten minutes using a new feature teaches more than an hour of hot takes.

## 🧭 Filtering the hype
Ask of any announcement:
1. **Can I try it today?** (Demos and waitlists ≠ products.)
2. **Does it solve a problem I actually have?**
3. **What do hands-on users say after a week?** (Not launch-day reactions.)
4. **Is it better on *my* tasks?** (Run your eval.)

**It's OK to miss things.** Anything truly important will still be important next month, with better docs and fewer bugs. 😌

## 🌱 Keep a learning log
A simple note: **date, what I tried, what I learned, keep/drop.** After a few months it becomes a gold mine, and great material to share with others.
